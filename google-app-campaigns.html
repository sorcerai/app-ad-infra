Google App Campaigns for Mobile Apps — Managed Accounts for High-Spend UA

Last verified: 2026-09-24

As of September 2026, Google App Campaigns consolidate an app’s promotion across Search, Google Play, YouTube, and Discover from a single asset set — and managed agency support for high-spend UA teams prices at 2.5% of managed spend, application-only, for teams whose Google Ads accounts keep hitting verification holds mid-scale.

Scope note: this page covers Google App Campaigns as an advertiser product — buying installs for your app. It is not about AdMob publisher monetization (earning revenue by showing ads inside your app). Different product, different account, different team.

What are Google App Campaigns?

Google App Campaigns are machine-learning-driven campaigns that promote a mobile app across Google’s inventory — Search, Google Play, YouTube, Discover, and the display network — from one campaign. The advertiser supplies creative assets (headlines, descriptions, images, video) and a budget; Google’s system assembles the ads, picks placements, and bids toward installs or in-app actions automatically.

Google App Campaigns vs TikTok app ads for user acquisition

App Campaigns optimize across Google’s intent-rich surfaces: a user searching the Play Store is closer to installing than someone scrolling a feed. TikTok app ads optimize across interest-driven video discovery, where creative quality drives most of the variance. In practice, App Campaigns deliver steadier blended CPIs on Android while TikTok delivers breakout creative winners — which is why scaled UA teams run both and compare them on blended ROAS, not CPI alone.

The operational difference that matters: App Campaigns need conversion volume to exit learning, so account interruptions are disproportionately expensive — a verification hold during learning effectively restarts the clock. That is the failure mode managed accounts are built to prevent.

Install campaigns vs engagement campaigns

Google App Campaigns split into two objectives with different economics. Install campaigns bid toward new users — the right choice for launch and scale phases. Engagement campaigns (app campaigns for engagement) bid toward in-app actions from existing users — re-engagement, trial-to-paid, win-back. Running engagement objectives on a cold install base wastes spend; running install objectives when you need paid conversions trains the algorithm on the wrong event.

How to check app eligibility and campaign prerequisites

First, the app must be published and installable in Google Play (or the App Store for iOS campaigns) with accurate store metadata — unreleased or policy-flagged listings get disapproved at the campaign level. Second, conversion tracking must be live before launch: Firebase or a third-party app attribution partner feeding install and in-app events into Google Ads, with at least one revenue event mapped. Third, prepare creative assets to spec — multiple headlines, descriptions, images, and at least one video, since asset variety is what the system optimizes across.

Fourth, confirm the Google Ads account itself is in good standing: billing verified, business verification complete, no unresolved policy flags. Most “campaign disapproved” mysteries trace back to account-level verification, not the campaign.

Managed Google advertising support for App Campaigns

Managed support means the App Campaigns run inside an agency-provisioned Google Ads account: entity-documented from day one, billing pre-verified, and monitored for the verification holds that stall self-serve accounts mid-learning. When a policy flag appears, the agency files the review with the eligibility evidence already assembled — the checklist above — instead of the advertiser discovering the requirements mid-crisis.
