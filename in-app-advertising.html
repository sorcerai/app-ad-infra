In-App Advertising vs App-Install Advertising — A Buyer's Guide

As of September 2026, the term in-app advertising covers two completely different buyer jobs: purchasing ad placements inside other people's apps, and buying installs for your own app. This guide is written for the advertiser — the buyer — and makes the advertiser/publisher split explicit so you buy the right product.

What is in-app advertising for an advertiser buying placements?

For an advertiser, in-app advertising means buying ad placements — banners, interstitials, rewarded video — inside third-party mobile apps to reach users where they spend their time. You are the buyer; the app developer is the publisher selling inventory, usually through an ad network or exchange. Success is measured on your campaign objective: installs, signups, or purchases — not on the publisher's monetization metrics.

In-app advertising vs app-install advertising

In-app advertising describes where the ad appears (inside apps); app-install advertising describes what the ad optimizes for (an install of your app). They overlap constantly — most app-install campaigns run as in-app placements — but they are not the same buying decision. You can run in-app placements optimized for purchases rather than installs, and you can buy installs through placements outside apps (mobile web, social feeds). Name the objective first, the placement second.

Buying placements vs monetizing your own app — the advertiser/publisher split

This is the split that confuses most searches. Buying placements = you pay to show ads inside other apps (demand side; this guide's audience). Monetizing your own app = you sell ad space inside your app to other advertisers (supply side; an AdMob/publishers topic this guide does not cover). If you own an app and want users, you are a buyer. If you own an app and want ad revenue, you are a publisher. The strategies, platforms, and metrics barely overlap.

In-app placement buying vs app-store search advertising

In-app placement buying (TikTok, Meta, ad networks) reaches users by interest and behavior, with creative doing the heavy lifting. App-store search advertising (Apple Search Ads, Google App Campaigns search inventory) reaches users by keyword intent at the moment they search the store. Placement buying scales broader and cheaper; search captures high-intent users at higher cost per install. Most mature UA programs run both, with search as the intent layer and placements as the scale layer.

Use this checklist. You are a buyer if: you have an app and want installs or in-app purchases; your KPI is CPI, CPA, or ROAS.
